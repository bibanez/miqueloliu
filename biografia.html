Miquel Oliu Nieto (Barcelona, 1973)

Musician, trained as a pianist and composer.

He was awarded the 32nd Reina Sofía Prize for Musical Composition (2015), for the work Al·legories de tardor (Autumn allegories) for symphony orchestra, premiered by the RTVE Symphony Orchestra, conducted by Carlos Kalmar. He was also the composer for the “Xarxa de Músiques” program of Joventuts Musicals de Catalunya – Fundació Ferrer-Salat during the 2016–17 academic year.

He studied at the Conservatorio Superior de Música de Aragón (Zaragoza) with Professor Agustí Charles, completing his Higher Degree in 2002.

In his development as a composer, his contact with Ramon Humet—under whom he studied regularly over a two-year period—was particularly significant (2010–2012).

He has attended various master classes and advanced courses, notably with Sofia Gubaidulina (2013) and Tristan Murail (2015) in Zaragoza, and with Tristan Murail again at the Barcelona Festival Mixtur (2019).

His catalogue includes works for solo instruments as well as chamber, choral, and orchestral ensembles. He has received numerous commissions from institutions and performers, among which the two works commissioned by L’Auditori de Barcelona for the OBC (Barcelona Symphony Orchestra) stand out: the first, In luce praesenti, premiered during the 2021–22 season of the OBC conducted by Juanjo Mena, and recorded in July 2022 by L’Auditori’s label (Auditori Play), with the OBC conducted by Benjamin Schwarz; the second, Pluja (Rain), premiered in the 2025–26 OBC season and subsequently toured in Germany and the Basque Country under its principal conductor Ludovic Morlot.

Also notable is the commission from the Palau de la Música Catalana, in collaboration with the Barcelona City Council, as part of the Barcelona Creació Sonora project: Tercer quadern de Preludis (Third book of preludes) for piano, with pianist Miquel Villalba (December 2023).

Miquel Oliu's music has been performed at venues across Spain, Germany, France, Portugal, Poland, Switzerland, and Japan: L'Auditori de Barcelona, the Palau de la Música Catalana, the Elbphilharmonie in Hamburg (Großer Saal), the Liederhalle in Stuttgart, the Tonhalle in Düsseldorf, the Kursaal in San Sebastián-Donostia, the Palacio Euskalduna in Bilbao, the Teatro Monumental and Juan March Foundation in Madrid, the Schubertíada in Vilabertran, the Auditori Josep Carreras in Vila-seca, the Teatre Atlàntida in Vic and the Monastery of Montserrat, among many others.

His works have been recorded on several CDs produced by Ficta (also his publisher), Seed Music, and IBS Classical. Notable among these is Llibre d'hores (The book of hours), a monographic piano album performed by Miquel Villalba (Ficta, 2023).

A significant portion of his work reflects his fondness for children, nature, and silence.
